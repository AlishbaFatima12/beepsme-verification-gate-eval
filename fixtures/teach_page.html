Chapter One: The Water Cycle

Water on Earth moves in a continuous loop called the water cycle. The sun heats water in oceans, lakes and rivers, and some of that water changes into vapour and rises into the air. This process is called evaporation. Plants also release water vapour from their leaves, which is called transpiration.

As the vapour rises it cools, and the cooling turns it back into tiny droplets of liquid water. This is condensation, and it is how clouds form. The droplets inside a cloud collide and join until they become too heavy to stay in the air.

When the drops fall, we call it precipitation. Depending on the temperature, precipitation reaches the ground as rain, snow, sleet or hail. Some of this water soaks into the soil, some flows into streams and rivers, and some is stored underground.

The cycle has no beginning and no end. A single drop of water may spend years in the ocean, weeks in a cloud, and only minutes as rain. Because the same water keeps moving through the cycle, the water we drink today is the same water that existed millions of years ago.

How Honeybees Find Their Way Home

A honeybee may fly several kilometres from its hive in search of flowers, yet it almost always finds its way back. For a creature with a brain smaller than a grain of rice, this is a remarkable feat of navigation.

Bees use the sun as a compass. Even on cloudy days, they can read the pattern of polarised light in the sky, which tells them where the sun is. As the sun moves during the day, the bee adjusts its course to match.

They also count. As a bee flies, the stream of ground detail passing beneath it acts like an odometer, letting it judge how far it has travelled. When it returns to the hive, it converts this distance into a dance that tells other bees where the flowers are.

Landmarks matter too. Bees remember trees, fences and buildings near the hive, and use them for the final approach, the way a pilot uses runway lights.

Scientists study bee navigation because it shows how a very small brain can solve a very hard problem with simple, reliable rules.
